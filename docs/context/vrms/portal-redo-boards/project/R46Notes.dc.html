Round 46: the map, checked

S18b was drawn by hand. Fitted against real coordinates, its pins were 8.4 km off on average, 19 km at worst, Pulilan to Sta. Rosa read as 60 km (really 71), and Laguna de Bay was half its size. The map on this page is still drawn, but traced: every shore, road and pin comes from OpenStreetMap coordinates, then cut down to the expressways and the main roads, smoothed into clean curves, the shores lined like an old chart, and the city shown as dots where the streets are dense. The twelve trips are routed on the real roads.

SUGGESTIONS FOR THE BUILD

  1. Credit OpenStreetMap in the footer. The map is traced from its data, so its licence (ODbL) asks for a credit where the map is shown. The map stays clean; "© OpenStreetMap", linked to its copyright page, sits in the page footer under the form, beside the copyright.
  2. Light enough to ship as is. The whole map is about 25 KB compressed (S18b 14 KB, the full street map 164 KB). Load it after the form paints, and animate only the truck layer above it.
  3. Never live data before sign in. The page is public: the trips stay a drawing of regular runs, never where the trucks really are.
  4. Calm when asked. Reduced motion parks every truck at its garage; pause the trucks when the tab is hidden.
  5. Phones get the form only (or a short map strip); the map starts at 1024 px wide.
  6. One title everywhere. The heading and the tab both say "WeLinc Logistics Operations Workspace" (tab: "Sign in · WeLinc Logistics Operations Workspace").
  7. Labels stay upright. In S18b they tilted with the map; here they sit flat on top and shrink with distance, so they read at a glance.
